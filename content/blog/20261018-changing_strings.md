Last Night I was changing the strings on an old Silvertone guitar that one of my wonderful big sisters in Christ gave me a while back. It is a very special guitar because it belonged to her late husband. As I was working on it, I was reminded of a time when I still had my hair and surfing and guitars filled my thoughts much more than they do today:

## Singing in the Fog

In 1993 and 1994 I lived in the Santa Cruz on the central coast of California. About once a month a group of young people from local churches and led by a pastor from Santa Cruz Bible Church, went down to the seawall area in Santa Cruz and sang praise songs together, often in the fog that so often shows up in that part of the coast after sunset.

Though I did not really now much about playing the guitar, I was in love with the instrument and wanted to learn. Of course, I could hardly play the simplest song, but my friends knew my passion and found a way to get me involved. I became the string changer.

Usually there where two or three guitars out with the guys pounding - not strumming but pounding - away and singing songs like "Lord I Lift Your Name on High" or "Make a Joyful Noise". The crowd was usually over a hundred at some point, many of whom were singing joyfully, some of whom were either confused or amused, and some of whom wanted us gone. 

Once the fog rolled in something strange began to happen: Strings would begin to break. The combination of cold, damp air and the pounding made the strings prone to go *boing*. And when I heard that sound, I had to move fast. I would need to get the guitar, pull the old string off, find the right new string, get it on, tighten it, stretch it, tune it, stretch it again, tune it again, and hand it back. I learned to get all that done in a minute or two before the next string broke.

It seems to me that one night I ended up changing five or six strings on two or three guitars during the evening. Perhaps that does not sound too exciting, but for me it was a huge joy because I got to handle guitars and receive gratitude from musicians.
