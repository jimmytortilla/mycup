I found a boook on line several years ago called <u>How to Memorize the Bible: Training the Memory to Learn Holy Scripture</u> by William Evans. It is a small booklet with time-tested steps to improving memory with scripture memorization in mind. The author himself had memorized the entire Bible in the King James Version as well as the New Testament in the American Standard Version.

<img src="/mycup/bible_memorization.png" alt="How to Memorize the Bible" style="display:block; width:min(88%, 24rem); max-width:100%; height:auto; margin:1.5rem auto; border-radius:8px;">

Over the years, I have started the book and then stopped a number of times - no fault of the book. Once again, I have begun to follow the steps; and this time I hope to stick with it.

The author gives six "essential" principles for successful memory training.  I am stil working on the first:  

### The First Essential Principle
##### Attention, or Fixity of Thought

The first step of mental exercise in the book for practicing fixity of thought is memorizing a list of words, looking for ways to connect the words. It is not difficult, in and of itself - I have tried it before - but it is difficult to continue the drill until it becomes a regular habit. So far I have committed to memory this list of 100 loosely-related words. Now I have to (get to) review them daily for the next month before moving on to the next step. 

### The Other Five Essential Principles

* ##### "A Clear and Definite Conception of the Matter to Be Memorized.
* ##### "The Analyzing of That Which You Desire to Memorize"
* ##### "Sugestive Association"
* ##### "The Power to Recal from Memory What You Have Learned"
* ##### "The Constant Pracitice of Reviewing"
